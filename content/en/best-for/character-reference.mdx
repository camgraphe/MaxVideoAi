## What this ranking measures
Character reference means preserving identity while pose, camera, location, or action changes. The best model must understand several references without blending faces, wardrobe, props, or voices.

## Best picks
1. **[Seedance 2.5](/models/seedance-2-5)** - best overall for large character reference packs, longer sequences, voice continuity, and targeted edits.
2. **[Kling O3 Pro](/models/kling-o3-pro)** - best for structured character and prop control across planned scene beats.
3. **[MiniMax H3](/models/minimax-h3)** - best for multimodal character, motion, and voice references with native stereo audio.
4. **[Wan 3 Prime](/models/wan-3-prime)** - best premium Wan route for reference-led character sequences and first/last-frame control.
5. **[Grok Imagine Video 1.5](/models/grok-imagine-video-1-5)** - best for short character-consistent clips guided by several reference images.

## Why these models lead now
Seedance 2.5 can assign many visual, video, and audio references to different jobs in the same generation. Kling O3 Pro remains the control-focused option. MiniMax H3 unifies subject, motion, style, and voice references, while Wan 3 Prime and Grok Imagine Video 1.5 give strong alternatives for shorter reference-led work.

## Character-reference checklist
- Give each image one job: face, wardrobe, prop, or style.
- Reuse the same short identity anchors in every planned beat.
- Keep one main action per shot.
- Separate voice guidance from visual guidance when the model supports both.
- Regenerate the failed beat instead of overloading the original prompt.

## FAQ
### What is the best model for character consistency?
Seedance 2.5 is the strongest broad choice. Kling O3 Pro is excellent for structured continuity, and MiniMax H3 is strong when voice, motion, and visual references must work together.

### How many references should I use?
Use only references with a clear purpose. More files help when roles are explicit; unrelated images increase ambiguity.

### Is a face image enough?
Not for strict continuity. Add full-body, wardrobe, and prop references when those details must survive camera and pose changes.
