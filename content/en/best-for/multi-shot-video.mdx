## Your scene comes first
Make the next shot feel like it belongs. Plan the cuts, then choose how to generate the sequence.

## The shortlist, in detail
1. **[Seedance 2.5](/models/seedance-2-5)** — Plan a sequence up to 30 seconds with a beginning, turns and an ending. Use references and explicit cuts to direct the sequence.
2. **[Kling 3.0 Omni Pro](/models/kling-o3-pro)** — A 1080p option for image-led scenes, reference-guided work and video transformation. Plan what each reference should control.
3. **[Wan 3 Prime](/models/wan-3-prime)** — Use opening and ending frames, mix references or extend a clip, with generation lengths up to 30 seconds.
4. **[MiniMax H3](/models/minimax-h3)** — Generate 5–15 second clips with audio, with output options up to 4K and support for image, video and audio references.
5. **[Wan 3](/models/wan-3)** — Start with text or an image, guide the scene with references, or continue an existing clip. Audio is optional.

## Questions before you create
### What is the best model for multi-shot video?
Try Seedance 2.5 for a sequence up to 30 seconds, then Kling Omni Pro when shot-by-shot direction matters most. Wan 3 Prime, H3 and Wan 3 offer other reference-led approaches. Write the cuts and action explicitly; a model can still merge or miss a shot.

### Is multi-shot the same as video editing?
No. Multi-shot generation creates several connected beats. Editing changes or assembles footage after generation, although Seedance 2.5 can also handle targeted edits.

### Which model gives the most explicit control?
For reference-led sequences, compare Kling O3 Pro, Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3 Prime. Describe each shot’s subject, action, camera and transition. No model contract guarantees every requested cut.
