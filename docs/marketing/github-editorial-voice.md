# MaxVideoAI GitHub editorial voice

MaxVideoAI writes like a capable producer: prepared, calm, and specific about
what happens next. The voice has subtle warmth, gives direct guidance, respects
the user's spend, and puts proof before superlatives.

Use customer verbs where they clarify an outcome: **plan, compare, budget,
quote, approve, generate, recover,** and **reuse**. Technical terms belong in
installation, architecture, and contributor material—not the first commercial
sentence.

## The working voice

- Lead with the production outcome, then show current proof or a concrete
  example.
- State a price, privacy, compatibility, or installation qualification beside
  the claim it changes. Do not open with a wall of caveats.
- Make the next action easy to recognize. A reader should know whether to
  install, compare, request a quote, approve, or recover work.
- Keep conversational turns light and earned. These are approved as written:
  “Bring the brief.”, “You stay in control of the spend.”, and “Pick up where
  the conversation left off.”

Avoid commercial shortcuts: `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `ultimate`,
`unleash`, `effortless magic`, urgency countdowns, emoji-led headings, invented
social proof, and competitive claims without evidence. Avoid unsupported
superlatives such as “best” or “most powerful”; replace them with a current
capability, an observed result, or a named qualification.

## Flat → MaxVideoAI

| Situation | Flat | MaxVideoAI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model choice | `Our AI picks the best model.` | `Compare current model options for each shot, then choose the one that fits the brief.` |
| Price | `Get affordable AI video.` | `See the exact project quote before a paid generation.` |
| Approval | `Click generate when ready.` | `Review the quote, approve the spend, then generate when the production is ready.` |
| Recovery | `Your jobs are saved.` | `If the conversation is interrupted, recover the completed generation from your MaxVideoAI library.` |
| Library continuity | `Everything stays in one place.` | `Reuse approved results and references from the same MaxVideoAI library.` |
| Installation | `Install our powerful MCP server.` | `Install MaxVideoAI in ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, or a compatible MCP client; platform steps are linked below.` |

## Qualification placement

Good qualifications stay near the affected promise:

- `Compare current models for every shot. Availability depends on the models
  enabled in your MaxVideoAI account.`
- `See the exact quote before generation. Prices reflect the selected model,
  duration, and current project settings.`
- `Use private references where the selected workflow supports them; review the
  permissions and privacy guide before connecting a host.`
- `Install in supported ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, or compatible MCP surfaces; use
  the platform-specific steps because host setup changes.`

Do not put all of those statements before the product outcome. Bring the
qualification to the claim only when it affects the reader's decision.

## Review questions

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does the copy tell a producer what they can do, rather than how an internal
   implementation works?
2. Does it name the proof, example, screenshot, price, or compatibility limit
   that makes the promise credible?
3. Could a calmer, more concrete sentence replace an adjective or a claim about
   competitors?
4. Does the reader keep control of the spend and know where the result lives?
